# ITIN & Form W-7 Guide for Non-Residents

A complete, plain-language guide to the Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN) — what it is, who needs one, which documents to send, how to certify a passport without mailing the original, how long the IRS takes, and why applications get rejected.

> **Disclaimer:** This guide is general guidance for information only. It is **not tax advice**, legal advice, or a substitute for advice about your specific situation. ITIN eligibility and filing requirements depend on your facts — consult a qualified tax professional, or have your application handled by an IRS Certified Acceptance Agent, before you file.

## What is an ITIN?

An ITIN — **Individual Taxpayer Identification Number** — is a US tax processing number issued by the IRS to individuals who need a US taxpayer identification number but are **not eligible for a Social Security Number (SSN)**.

An ITIN is:

- A **personal** tax ID, issued to one individual
- Used for US federal tax filing and reporting
- Issued by applying on **Form W-7**

An ITIN is **not** a work permit, does not confer immigration status, and is not an SSN.

## Who needs an ITIN?

You may need an ITIN if you are a non-resident (or another individual without SSN eligibility) who must interact with the US tax system, for example to:

- **File a US tax return** — including returns connected to a US LLC or other US-source income
- **Open certain accounts** that require a US tax ID
- **Receive payments** where a payer, platform, marketplace, or withholding agent requires a US tax ID for reporting or withholding

The common founder case: a non-resident founder of a US LLC who has a US filing obligation but no SSN. The ITIN is the personal number that makes that filing possible.

## The Form W-7 document set

A complete W-7 application has three parts. Sending only the W-7 form by itself is a common cause of rejection.

### 1. Form W-7 itself

- Completed and **signed**
- With the correct **reason box** checked — the reason you are applying must match your documentation

### 2. Tax return or exception documentation

- In the standard case, the **US federal tax return** the ITIN is needed for, attached to the W-7
- **Or**, where an exception applies instead of a tax return, the **documentation for that exception**

One or the other must support the application — a W-7 with neither a tax return nor exception documentation does not establish why the number is needed.

### 3. Proof of identity + foreign status

- Documents proving **both** your identity **and** your foreign status
- **A passport alone stands for both** — a valid passport is the one document that proves identity and foreign status on its own
- If you do not use a passport, you will generally need a combination of documents to cover both identity and foreign status

## Passport certification routes — do not mail your original if you can avoid it

The IRS needs to be satisfied your identity document is genuine. There are three routes:

### Route A — IRS Acceptance Agent / Certified Acceptance Agent (recommended)

- Apply through an IRS **Acceptance Agent (AA)** or **Certified Acceptance Agent (CAA)**
- A CAA can **certify** your passport (and certain other identity documents) — the agent verifies the original and certifies a copy for the IRS
- **This is how you avoid mailing your original passport:** the certified copy goes to the IRS, and your original stays with you
- **Advice: use certified copies via a CAA rather than mailing originals to the IRS**

### Route B — IRS Taxpayer Assistance Centers (TACs), by appointment

- Certain IRS **Taxpayer Assistance Centers** can authenticate identity documents in person
- This is **by appointment** — book ahead, and confirm the specific center offers this service before you travel

### Route C — Mail originals to the IRS (Austin)

- Mail your application, with your **original** passport (or other original documents), to the **IRS Austin service center** in Texas
- Your originals are at risk in transit and out of your hands while the IRS processes the application
- Use this route only if the CAA or TAC routes are not available to you — and if you do mail originals, use a trackable, secure service

## IRS processing time

- ITIN applications are typically processed in **7–11 weeks**
- Plan around that timeline: do not leave a W-7 until just before a filing deadline that depends on the ITIN
- Processing can only start from a **complete** application — an incomplete application does not hold your place in the queue

## Common rejection reasons

Applications are rejected or suspended most often for avoidable paperwork problems:

1. **Missing or inconsistent documents** — the tax return / exception documentation is missing, or details (name, date of birth, passport details) do not match across documents
2. **Unsigned W-7** — the Form W-7 was not signed
3. **Wrong reason box** — the reason checked on the W-7 does not match the tax return or exception documentation supplied
4. **Expired passport** — the passport used for identity / foreign status had expired
5. **Photocopies not certified** — plain photocopies were sent where certified copies (via a CAA) or originals were required

Before sending, check: signed W-7, correct reason box, matching details everywhere, a valid (unexpired) passport, and certification handled through a CAA / TAC — or originals, if you are using the mail route.

## After approval — the CP565 notice

- If your application is approved, the IRS assigns your ITIN and sends you a **CP565 notice** confirming it
- Keep the CP565 safe — it is your record of the number
- Use the ITIN on the US tax filings it was issued for

## Renewal — ITINs expire if unused

- An ITIN **expires if it is not used for 3 consecutive years**
- An expired ITIN needs to be **renewed** before it is used on a new filing
- If you have an ITIN you have not used in several years, check whether it is still valid before you rely on it for a return

## ITIN vs EIN — do not confuse them

| | ITIN | EIN |
|---|---|---|
| What it identifies | A **person** | A **business** |
| Full name | Individual Taxpayer Identification Number | Employer Identification Number |
| Form | Form **W-7** | Form **SS-4** |
| Typical founder use | The founder's personal US tax filings | The LLC's own tax filings, banking, and business identification |

> **An ITIN is a personal tax ID; an EIN is for the business.** A non-resident founder often needs both: an EIN for the LLC, and an ITIN for themselves.
>
> **Non-resident EIN timing:** an EIN for a non-resident (no SSN / no US tax ID for the responsible party) is obtained by **faxed SS-4** and takes **12–18 days**.

## Quick checklist

- [ ] Form W-7 completed, correct reason box, **signed**
- [ ] US tax return attached — or exception documentation instead
- [ ] Valid, unexpired passport (proves identity **and** foreign status on its own)
- [ ] Passport certified via a CAA, authenticated at a TAC by appointment, or originals mailed to the IRS Austin service center
- [ ] All names and details consistent across every document
- [ ] Submitted with **7–11 weeks** of IRS processing time in your plan
- [ ] CP565 notice filed safely when it arrives
